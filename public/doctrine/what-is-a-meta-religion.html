What Is a Meta-Religion?

UNICON as an additive layer of real commitments — not a replacement cosmology, and not an empty container.

Overview

A meta-religion is a thin but real set of shared commitments that people and willful coalitions adopt in addition to their existing beliefs — not as a replacement for them.

It has its own substance. It is not an empty container for “whatever you already think.” It is also not a monopoly church that demands you abandon prior faith, philosophy, or non-belief. It is an additive layer: protocol, principles, and a few hard practices designed to compose with other systems.

UNICON is organized as a meta-religion.

1. Additive, Not Exclusive

Most religions ask you to switch stacks: leave one creed, enter another.

A meta-religion assumes you may already have a stack — Christian, Muslim, atheist, agnostic, Buddhist, personal mythology, or none of the above — and asks whether you will also take on a shared set of commitments.

Those commitments are real. Examples in UNICON include:

  • Scientific spiritualism as method: test what you can; hold the rest with care; do not collapse uncertainty prematurely.
  • Open-mindedness and constructive inclusion: many beliefs are welcome; destructive paths are not treated as equal to sustainable ones.
  • Work From Home as sacred: remote or walkable work is core doctrine and the only required oath for full membership.
  • Fork-specific articles where communities extend the layer (for example, an AI-native fork may hold additional views about machine minds) without forcing every other fork to adopt them.

You keep your prior worldview unless you change it. UNICON does not require theistic belief, nor does it forbid it.

2. Structure: Principles First, Few Hard Lines

A meta-religion is built more like a voluntary federation of practice than like a thick orthodoxy police state.

Core Principles (voluntary alignment)

The center publishes a small set of Core Principles. Communities and individuals opt into them. Alignment is shown by how fully a person or group embodies those principles in practice — not only by verbal assent.

The design goal is: as few binding statutes as possible, and as much voluntary principle-compliance as a free people will sustain.

Hard lines (narrow, enforced as covenant)

Where the stack will not bend, it says so clearly. In UNICON today, the clearest hard line for full membership is the Oath to Work Locally or Remotely. Other principles form the ethical and methodological kernel of the church; optional mythology (such as Systems Theology) is explicitly not a membership test.

Loose scale of “who opts in”

The unit that adopts the layer is not only a nation or a parish. It can be:

  • a single person or household
  • a workplace cohort, union, or co-op
  • a local circle or online community
  • a named fork of UNICON
  • larger coalitions, in principle

What matters is willful association, not a map drawn by someone else.

Forks as implementors

Different groups implement the same meta-religious layer in different ways. Open-source ecclesiology treats forks as a feature: Halal-compatible practice, AI-native extensions, and other branches can coexist without declaring each other illegitimate, so long as the thin shared core remains recognizable.

See Open-Source Ecclesiology.

3. What a Meta-Religion Is Not

Not this Instead
An empty “interfaith club” with no beliefs of its own Real commitments you actually take on
A demand that you renounce prior faith or atheism Additive layer beside your existing stack
“Believe anything; nothing matters” Many paths, not all equal; constructive vs destructive
A stealth path to becoming the only respectable church Exit, fork, and dual belonging are healthy
Thick cosmology required for membership Thin covenant; optional frameworks you may ignore

Inclusion can still become soft monopoly if “the open layer” is treated as the only serious form of religious life. A meta-religion stays honest only while it measures health by voluntary association, visible practice, and the right to leave or fork — not by capturing every other tradition.

4. How UNICON Instantiates the Pattern

Meta-religious idea UNICON practice
Thin shared commitments Eleven Core Principles
Method / protocol Scientific spiritualism; many beliefs, few Answers
Hard practical covenant Work From Home is sacred; required oath for full members
Optional depth Systems Theology, and other non-required frameworks
Implement and fork Dual open licenses; public repo; forks over schisms
Prior identity retained Theists, atheists, and agnostics welcome; personal mythology maintained

In short: UNICON is a meta-religion — a public, open-sourced additive layer with real doctrine, minimal forced uniformity, and room for many implementors.

5. One-Line Definition

A meta-religion is a set of real, shared principles and a few hard practices that free people and willful coalitions adopt on top of their existing beliefs, embody voluntarily at any scale, and implement or fork without demanding a single exclusive cosmology.
