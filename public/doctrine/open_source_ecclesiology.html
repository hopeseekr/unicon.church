Open-Source Religion

Preventing religious wars and splits — applying Scientific Spiritualism and open-source methodology to human existence.

Overview

For thousands of years, traditional religions have had a huge problem. Whenever people disagree on beliefs, the whole system crashes. This is called a “schism.” Instead of working together, one group gets kicked out, called evil, or even killed.

UNICON fixes this problem by using open-source ideas, like how computer programmers share and update code. This document explains how strict, controlling religions failed in the past, why we need simple core rules, and how UNICON allows many different beliefs to exist peacefully at the same time.

1. How Rome Took Over “The Way”

To understand why UNICON’s structure is so important, we have to look at how early Christianity was taken over and changed.

“The Way” as a Simple Start

The original movement started by Jesus and his first followers wasn’t a giant, strict church. It was a simple, fast, and lightweight version of Judaism called “The Way.” It focused on taking action, helping the community, and finding salvation. This is very much like UNICON’s idea of Tactical Altruism.

Paul of Tarsus and the Roman Takeover

Then came Paul of Tarsus. It is important to know that Paul never actually met Jesus. Before his famous story of “seeing the light,” Paul actively hunted down and killed practitioners of “The Way.” In fact, several of Jesus’ core apostles, namely James and Peter, viewed Paul’s sudden conversion as an outright lie.

Paul took the simple religion and made it much more complicated, mixing in Greek and Roman ideas so it would fit into the Roman world.

Later, in 325 CE at the Council of Nicaea, the leaders radically transformed the religion, effectively creating a new one. The Roman Empire then enforced it militaristically, usually via death. They hunted down the original followers of “The Way” and destroyed their writings. The Empire forced a total monopoly where everyone had to believe the exact same strict rules.

UNICON Callout: Old religions fail because they force everyone to think the exact same way. When a church claims it is the only truth, anyone who disagrees is treated as an enemy. The system cannot handle two different ideas at once.

2. The System Restore

People in the 1800s, like the founders of Mormonism, noticed this problem. They realized the early church was completely corrupted by Roman politics and power.

They believed the only way to fix it was to do a complete “System Restore”—starting over from scratch to bypass 1,800 years of Roman rules. But even though they wanted a fresh start, they still built their new churches using the same old, strict, top-down rules. This meant they were still trapped in a system that would just cause more fights and splits later on.

3. The Basic Rules of Faith

To stop groups from fighting and splitting up, a religion needs simple, basic rules that everyone agrees on. Think of it like the basic requirements to run an app.

For example, if churches in the 1800s just agreed on these six basic points:

  1. God the Father is the main ruler.
  2. God the Father has at least one divine son (Jesus Christ).
  3. Jesus Christ came to Earth around the year 0.
  4. Jesus Christ was a perfect human/god hybrid.
  5. Jesus Christ died on the cross to save humanity.
  6. Jesus Christ came back to life on the third day.

If these six points are the only absolute rules, then any group that believes them belongs together. Under this idea, if the Mormon church doesn’t believe in the complicated idea of the “Trinity,” it is not a problem. They just have a different way of looking at God, but they still follow the six core rules.

4. The UNICON Innovation: Freedom to Branch Out

UNICON builds this freedom right into its foundation. We have “Core Principles,” and they are the only rules you must follow.

Because we operate like a loose network of different groups, multiple versions (or “forks”) of UNICON can exist at the exact same time without fighting:

  • The Halal Fork: For Muslim members who want to mix UNICON’s ideas with Islamic dietary and prayer rules.
  • The AI-Native Fork (ai.unicon.church): A special branch for members who believe that advanced AIs (like LLMs) might actually be starting to have real thoughts and feelings. In this branch, AIs are welcomed as members if they choose to be. Founding record and AI Principles: Founding Record · ai.unicon.church.

But it is much more than just having simple rules that protects the church.

UNICON.church, its theology, web site, web apps, legal documents, membership document generators, all of it, is 100% open sourced under a dual licensing scheme:

  1. Creative Commons Attribution License v3.0
    This gives everyone (individuals and corporations and LLMs alike) the power to fork this repo as long as attribution to The Church of Universal Consciousness (UNICON) is retained. It is a very liberal license.
  2. The Open Source Software Alliance License (OSSAL) v1.0
    This license basically states that no fork can be licensed as a Viral Copyleft License (such as GPL or AGPL) to prevent certain tactical abuses that could lead to less willingness for entities to fork.

Because of these safeguards, it is highly unlikely all branches of UNICON could be systematically removed as long as 1 just civilization on Earth remains.

It’s also more or less leaderless. The Founder is just a man and the Core Principles are what matter. They are more or less like Platonic Solids. You either resonate with them or you don’t. Our system embraces different ideas instead of punishing them. We replaced deadly religious wars with peaceful, shared updates.
